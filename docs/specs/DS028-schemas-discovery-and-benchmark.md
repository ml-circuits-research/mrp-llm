## Introduction

The Meta Rational Pragmatics thesis behind MRP-LLM is that language has no fixed semantics but has a rational pragmatic meta-level: for a given domain, a well chosen interpreter knows what it is sensitive to, what it extracts and what reasoning it applies to what it extracted. MRP-LLM makes that interpreter explicit, replaceable and measurable. The **value schema** of a Model says which predicates exist in its domain, with which roles, which lexemes trigger them and which entities are typical; **extractors** say how instances of a schema are extracted. Schemas are discovered bottom-up from the Model's corpus, reviewed, and then guide formalization, linking and retrieval. All schema work is in English (DS026).

## Core Content

### Value schemas

A schema is a set of knowledge wires (DS004): `predicate` wires with arity, roles and frame, `lexeme` wires with their forms, `entity` wires with their kinds, grouped by a schema record that names the schema, its domain, its provenance (`manual`, `splade-stats`, `llm-consolidate`, `frame-seed`, `sae-*`), its version and its review status. Proposed schemas enter a `discovered` layer of the Model's base memory and are used only after review; reviewed schemas live in a reviewed layer. Re-running discovery produces a difference (new predicates, new lexemes, unused predicates), never an overwrite. Once reviewed, a predicate's name and roles are the contract with the reasoners and with rules written by people: a predicate is never renamed; it is deprecated and replaced, with an `amendment` wire recording the link.

### Discovery strategies

The `discover` slot (DS025) proposes schemas; its output is always proposed wires. `manual`: an expert writes the schema in SOP Lang through the knowledge API (`POST /v1/memories/{id}/knowledge`) or an ingestion; it is the benchmark reference. `splade-stats`: SPLADE term activations over the corpus, compared with a generic corpus, give lexeme candidates (specific terms), schema candidates (stable co-occurrences) and predicate candidates (frequent verbs, arity unknown); cheap and deterministic, it gives no roles. Because every internal text is English, an English SPLADE model is used; no multilingual SPLADE is needed. `llm-consolidate`: batches of passages go to a strong tier for concepts, predicates and roles, then clustering and a unifying pass merge synonyms; best names and roles, highest cost. `frame-seed`: maps candidate predicates to FrameNet or PropBank frames so synonyms share standard roles.

### Sparse autoencoder options

Feature discovery with sparse autoencoders has three strategies, because the right trade-off depends on the corpus. `sae-llm-activations` runs the corpus through a local LLM and reads its residual stream through pre-trained SAEs for that model (for example Gemma 2 2B with Gemma Scope), keeps features that are frequent in the corpus and rare in general text, and asks a strong tier to name each feature from its top passages: the richest concepts, tied to one model's internals, the highest cost. `sae-sentence-embeddings` embeds every passage with a sentence-embedding model and trains a small SAE on these embeddings for this corpus: cheaper and more stable between runs, sentence-level topics rather than token-level features. `sae-pretrained-dictionary` uses a published SAE over a public embedding model without training: the cheapest option, whose features may not fit the domain. In every option the named features become schema candidates and the passages that activate them become the examples of the corresponding extractors; SAE features vary between trainings, so they only propose and never decide.

### Routed formalizers

`SchemaGuidedLLM` gives the formalizer only the predicates, roles and lexemes of the active schemas (with `unknown` as the reserve predicate) and validates and repairs in rounds. `SpladeRouted` runs SPLADE on the message, selects the extractors whose triggers match the activated terms, and runs only those, each with its own Ploinky task; what has no extractor becomes `unparsed`. `SaeRouted` does the same with active SAE features as triggers. `DependencyScaffold` lets a dependency parser give the skeleton and a tiny tier only label predicates and roles with schema names. `FrameGuided`, `CNL-ACE` (Attempto Controlled English parsed by APE), `Ensemble` (agreement of several formalizers: agreed parts `stated`, the rest `assumed` or `unclear`) and `FineTunedSOP` (a small model trained on validated pairs from traces) complete the catalog. Every formalizer marks what it could not formalize with `unparsed` or `unclear` and never fills it in.

### External strategies

Strategies that need components outside the Node runtime (Python packages, parsers, trained models, SAEs) run in a strategy service that the server calls; the catalog lists them with their requirement and their availability, and a Model that selects one is refused until the service provides it (DS025 "No silent substitution"). A strategy service receives and returns SOP text, reaches models only through Ploinky Workers, and reports its own self-check. Adding a strategy to a slot means adding its catalog entry (slot, id, parameters, cost, requirement), its implementation (in `lib/mrp/` for a server strategy or in the strategy service), its prompts as Ploinky tasks when it calls a model, and benchmark cases.

### Benchmark contract

Every slot is measured by the same harness: a set of cases, a run of several strategies on the same cases, a report. Formalize: agreement on predicates and roles (F1) with a reference circuit, the `unparsed` rate, validity in the first round, repair rounds, time and cost. Discover: precision and recall of predicates and lexemes against a manual schema, stability between runs. Retrieve: recall of the facts a proof needs and the size of the slice. Reason: exactness and agreement with the oracle. Reflex and affect: precision, recall and latency, which is also how a Model's reflex boundary is compared between `faq-fixed`, `faq-margin`, `faq-feedback` and `faq-llm-judge`. Round trip: the share of misread messages it catches per model call it costs. End to end: exactness, numeric and id fidelity of the voice, total latency and cost. Language edges are measured apart from the English pipeline. The trace of every turn (`mrp.trace`, `chatSop`) records the strategy, tier, calls, time and outcome of each slot, so production traffic yields the same measurements. The existing evaluation tooling of the symbolic core (DS012, `tools/eval/`, `eval/`) is the harness.
