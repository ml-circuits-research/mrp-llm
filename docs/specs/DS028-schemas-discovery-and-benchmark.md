## Introduction

The Meta Rational Pragmatics thesis behind MRP-LLM is that language has no fixed semantics but has a rational pragmatic meta-level: for a given domain, a well chosen interpreter knows what it is sensitive to, what it extracts and what reasoning it applies to what it extracted. MRP-LLM makes that interpreter explicit, replaceable and measurable. The **value schema** of a Model says which predicates exist in its domain, with which roles, which lexemes trigger them and which entities are typical; **extractors** say how instances of a schema are extracted. Schemas are discovered bottom-up from the Model's corpus, reviewed, and then guide formalization, linking and retrieval. All schema work is in English (DS026).

## Core Content

### Value schemas

A schema is a `schema` wire (DS004 grammar, help page `docs/wire_typs/schema.html`): its `name`, `domain`, the `predicate ~id` lines of the predicates it groups, the `lexeme ~id` lines that trigger them, the `entity_type` kinds it is about, its `provenance` (`manual`, `splade-stats`, `llm-consolidate`, `frame-seed`, `sae-*`), `version` and `status` (`proposed`, `reviewed`, `deprecated`; default `reviewed`). The validator checks that every member exists and has the right type (`unknown_ref`, `schema_member_type`). An `extractor` wire (`docs/wire_typs/extractor.html`) is the extraction recipe of a schema: the formalize strategy, the triggers, the Ploinky task and the expected output wire types. The lexicon of a memory reads the schemas (`Lexicon.schemas`, `reviewedSchemaPredicates()`, kept by the compiled lexicon cache); the engines skip both wire types. The seed memory `acme-demo` carries a reviewed manual schema (`config/knowledge/acme-demo/0002-schema.sop`). Proposed schemas wait in the Model's review queue and are used only after review. Re-running discovery produces a difference (new predicates, known ones, unused ones), never an overwrite. Once reviewed, a predicate's name and roles are the contract with the reasoners and with rules written by people: a predicate is never renamed; it is deprecated and replaced, with an `amendment` wire recording the link.

### Discovery strategies

The `discover` slot (DS025) proposes schemas; its output is always proposed wires. `manual`: an expert writes the schema in SOP Lang through the knowledge API (`POST /v1/memories/{id}/knowledge`) or an ingestion; it is the benchmark reference. `splade-stats` (in the strategy service, "External strategies"): SPLADE term activations over the corpus, compared with a generic prior, give lexeme candidates (specific terms) and predicate candidates (verbal terms, arity unknown); cheap and deterministic, it gives no roles, so its candidates are listed in the response for review and never written as wires. `term-stats` computes the same specificity from plain term frequencies, with no package: the baseline of `splade-stats` and the check of the service. Because every internal text is English, an English SPLADE model is used; no multilingual SPLADE is needed. `llm-consolidate` (a server strategy, `lib/mrp/discover.mjs`, run by `POST /v1/mrp/models/{id}/discover` with `{passages, strategy?, params?}`): batches of passages (`batchChars`, default 12000) go to a strong tier (default `good`, purpose `job:discover`) that names the recurring relations with roles, role kinds, a description and English phrases; candidates are merged by name, one unifying call merges synonyms of equal arity, and each survivor becomes a `predicate` wire with its `lexeme` wire (role kinds only when the memory has that class). Every candidate is checked by the knowledge validator against the Model's base memory; each valid one becomes one item of the Model's review queue (`kind schema`, `source discover:llm-consolidate`), approved into the base memory like committed session knowledge, and one more item (`kind schema-record`) holds the `schema` wire of the run with `status proposed`, written as `reviewed` when it is approved after its predicates. The extraction is the batchable Ploinky task `mrp-discover-extract` (several batches may share one request) and the unification the task `mrp-discover-unify`. A candidate whose name or phrase the memory already has is reported as `known`, invalid ones as `invalid` with their problem codes, and the memory's predicates the corpus did not name as `unused`; best names and roles, highest cost. `frame-seed`: maps candidate predicates to FrameNet or PropBank frames so synonyms share standard roles.

### Sparse autoencoder options

Feature discovery with sparse autoencoders has three strategies, because the right trade-off depends on the corpus. `sae-llm-activations` runs the corpus through a local LLM and reads its residual stream through pre-trained SAEs for that model (for example Gemma 2 2B with Gemma Scope), keeps features that are frequent in the corpus and rare in general text, and asks a strong tier to name each feature from its top passages: the richest concepts, tied to one model's internals, the highest cost. `sae-sentence-embeddings` embeds every passage with a sentence-embedding model and trains a small SAE on these embeddings for this corpus: cheaper and more stable between runs, sentence-level topics rather than token-level features. `sae-pretrained-dictionary` uses a published SAE over a public embedding model without training: the cheapest option, whose features may not fit the domain. In every option the named features become schema candidates and the passages that activate them become the examples of the corresponding extractors; SAE features vary between trainings, so they only propose and never decide.

### Routed formalizers

`DirectSOP` and `SchemaGuidedLLM` are server strategies (`lib/formalize/strategies.mjs`, the one-shot author restored from ChatSOP's archive into `lib/query-author/one-shot/` with its guide `skills/coding-agent-query/`): one call on the strategy's tier writes the whole circuit, the validator's problems go back for at most three repair rounds, and a reply cut by its token limit is a failure, never a circuit. `DirectSOP` offers the retrieved candidate predicates and may open the vocabulary dialog (at most two expansions). `SchemaGuidedLLM` closes the vocabulary: until schema records group predicates, the schema of a Model is the predicate vocabulary of its base memory; the prompt offers those predicates with their roles, a session `predicate`, `rule`, `default`, `aggregate` or `assumed` wire goes back for repair (`schema_closed`), and anything no predicate expresses is `unparsed` or `unclear kind relation_not_in_memory`. The chat's default stays step by step (owner decision 2026-10-02); the archived names `LLMDirect`, `CodingAgent` and `LocalLLMDirect` stay archived. `SpladeRouted` runs SPLADE on the message, selects the extractors whose triggers match the activated terms, and runs only those, each with its own Ploinky task; what has no extractor becomes `unparsed`. `SaeRouted` does the same with active SAE features as triggers. `DependencyScaffold` lets a dependency parser give the skeleton and a tiny tier only label predicates and roles with schema names. `FrameGuided`, `CNL-ACE` (Attempto Controlled English parsed by APE), `Ensemble` (DirectSOP, SchemaGuidedLLM and LocalLLMStepByStep in parallel: two circuits that agree by signature win, no agreement is a doubt signal for the round trip), `EnsembleOneShot` (the same with the two one-shot authors only), `SchemaOrDirect` (SchemaGuidedLLM when the memory has a reviewed schema, DirectSOP otherwise, recorded as `chosen`) and `FineTunedSOP` (a small model trained on validated pairs from traces) complete the catalog. Every formalizer marks what it could not formalize with `unparsed` or `unclear` and never fills it in.

### External strategies

Strategies that need Python run in the MRP strategy service, `services/strategy-service/server.py` (standard library for the protocol; a strategy imports its packages only when it runs; listening on 127.0.0.1, port `MRP_STRATEGY_SERVICE_PORT`, default 18090; its address for the server is `MRP_STRATEGY_SERVICE_URL` or `mrp.strategyService.url` of the runtime configuration). Its API: `GET /selfcheck` lists every strategy with `available` and the missing packages; `POST /run {slot, strategy, input, params}` answers `{ok, output, trace}` or `{ok: false, error}`. The service never calls a language model. A catalog entry with `service: true` is available exactly when the self-check says so (`lib/mrp/strategy-service.mjs`, cached 30 s); a Model that selects an unavailable one is refused with the reason (DS025 "No silent substitution"). Today the service holds `discover/term-stats` and `discover/splade-stats` (transformers and torch). The other external entries are documented designs, catalogued `not-implemented`: `DependencyScaffold`, `FrameGuided`, `SpladeRouted`, `SaeRouted`, `CNL-ACE`, `FineTunedSOP`, `schema-linker`, `splade-index`, `dense-index`, `splade-slice`, `graph-walk`, `hybrid-rerank`, `embedding`, `frame-seed` and the three SAE options. `graph-walk` and `hybrid-rerank` wait for a corpus memory: the slice retrieval of the core is demand-driven by the query's predicates and the closure of the rules, so a walk over neighbouring facts adds nothing a proof needs on the memories of today. Compilation stays inside the router (one compiler per engine); a per-Model compile plugin is not a slot. Adding a strategy to a slot means adding its catalog entry (slot, id, parameters, cost, requirement), its implementation (in `lib/mrp/` for a server strategy or in the strategy service), its prompts as Ploinky tasks when it calls a model, and benchmark cases.

### Benchmark contract

Every slot is measured by the same harness: a set of cases, a run of several strategies on the same cases, a report. Formalize: agreement on predicates and roles (F1) with a reference circuit, the `unparsed` rate, validity in the first round, repair rounds, time and cost. Discover: precision and recall of predicates and lexemes against a manual schema, stability between runs. Retrieve: recall of the facts a proof needs and the size of the slice. Reason: exactness and agreement with the oracle. Reflex and affect: precision, recall and latency, which is also how a Model's reflex boundary is compared between `faq-fixed`, `faq-margin`, `faq-feedback` and `faq-llm-judge`. Round trip: the share of misread messages it catches per model call it costs. End to end: exactness, numeric and id fidelity of the voice, total latency and cost. Language edges are measured apart from the English pipeline. The trace of every turn (`mrp.trace`, `chatSop`) records the strategy, tier, calls, time and outcome of each slot, so production traffic yields the same measurements. The existing evaluation tooling of the symbolic core (DS012, `tools/eval/`, `eval/`) is the harness. The MRP slots have their own runners under `tools/eval/mrp/`: `slots.mjs` (every reflex strategy on `eval/mrp-slots-v1/cases.jsonl`: right, wrong, intrusive, missed, precision, recall; every affect strategy on the same file or, with `--set pragmatics`, on the 300 labelled messages of `eval/pragmatics-v1` in several languages, with `--normalize` choosing the normalize strategy before it: accuracy, macro precision and recall, emotion detection), `formalize.mjs` (every formalize strategy on `eval/mrp-formalize-v1`: 40 acme-demo questions with hand gold, 6 of them unanswerable, the 32 commonsense questions, and `acme-ro`, 16 of the acme questions in Romanian run behind a normalize strategy; correct, wrong, honest unknown, parser failed, accuracy, abstention, latency, and the model calls and tokens counted from Ploinky's request log by run tag), `roundtrip.mjs` (the catch rate of misread circuits and the false alarms on faithful ones, built from a formalize run, with a threshold sweep of the lexical comparison) and `voice.mjs` (the share of phrasings that pass the voice's fidelity guard, per strategy and language). Strategies that call a model run only live. The measured results and the defaults chosen from them are in `eval/reports/history/mrp-strategies-2026-10-14.md`.
