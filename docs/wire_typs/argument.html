argument

A reason for or against a wire or an amendment, with its source and an optional cost difference. Evidence for the negotiation of a change, never evidence of a fact.

Status and author: Knowledge wire, written by an author or composed by the host from the user's words. Not governed. It is never used to derive an answer; it is shown with the amendment it concerns.

Accepted fields

Single means at most once, repeatable means zero or more, and required means at least once. Other field names are rejected (unknown_field). The table is generated from the grammar in sop/knowledge/grammar.mjs.

FieldCardinalityAccepted values and behavior
forAt most once; optionalThe $wire_or_amendment this argument supports. Exactly one of for and against (argument_needs_for_or_against).
againstAt most once; optionalThe $wire_or_amendment this argument opposes.
claimAt most once; requiredThe reason, JSON-quoted.
sourceAt most once; optionalQuoted citation of where the reason comes from.
cost_deltaAt most once; optionalAn integer: the change in plan cost the argument claims for the proposal.

What it is for and how to write it

Write one argument per reason, pointing at the wire or amendment it concerns and quoting its source. An argument has exactly one of for and against.

What the engine does

Nothing in an answer: arguments are stored with the amendment they concern, listed when the host presents the delta of a what-if, and kept (with a rejected amendment) so that the same proposal is not re-litigated blindly. Dung-style argumentation between amendments and norms is not part of the language.

Examples

@a predicate
  args subject:entity
@act action
  params ?x
  requires a ?x
  adds a ?x
@n1 norm
  forbid ~act ?x
  version 1
@n2 norm
  forbid ~act ?x
  when a ?x
  version 2
  supersedes $n1
  approval proposed
@arg1 argument
  for $n2
  claim "the narrower prohibition still covers the risk"
  source "incident review 2026-09"
  cost_delta -2

While the successor is only proposed the predecessor stays approved; an approved successor would require the predecessor to be marked approval superseded (superseded_not_marked).

Invalid: both for and against

@a predicate
  args subject:entity
@act action
  params ?x
  requires a ?x
  adds a ?x
@n1 norm
  forbid ~act ?x
@arg1 argument
  for $n1
  against $n1
  claim "x"

Common mistakes

  1. Writing both for and against, or neither.
  2. Using an argument to assert a fact: write a fact with its source.
  3. Quoting a paraphrase as the source.
