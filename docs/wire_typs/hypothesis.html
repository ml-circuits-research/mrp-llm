hypothesis

A hypothesis holds possible ground assumptions for abduction or intervention. It is a candidate, not a stored fact or a proof. Trusted system/tool circuits may author it; model-authored declarative input cannot.

SOP syntax: @id type, exactly two leading spaces on each field line, $id for a computed value, ~id for a declaration handle, and ?x for a local logic variable or an output name. Atoms use [not ]predicate term1 [term2 [term3 [term4]]] with one to four whitespace-separated terms; lowercase predicate names, canonical symbols, JSON-quoted strings or safe integers. Parenthesized predicate(arg, …) and comma-separated arguments are not executable syntax.

Fields and inputs

KeywordCardinalityAccepted value, default and effect
holdsAt most once; optionalOne optional ground atom, e.g. disk_full server. At least one holds or assume must be present.
assumeRepeatable; optionalRepeatable ground atom; combines with holds into one hypothesis whose assumptions are tested together.
costAt most once; optionalFinite nonnegative number, default 1. Abduction orders subsets by summed cost; planning action costs are separate.
statusAt most once; optionaluntested (default), supported or rejected. Rejected candidates are skipped by abduction; supported is metadata, not admission.
sourceAt most once; optionalPlain/quoted provenance text, default provided.

Supply a ~hypothesis or $hypothesis (or packed array) to abduce candidates, diagnose candidates, analogize transfer or simulate intervention. Generic data may supply hypotheses for abduction/diagnosis. plan does not accept hypotheses as actions.

Result and limits

A typed object with ground assumptions, cost and status; no world write. Abduction generates fresh hypothetical explanations and never promotes the candidate into an admitted fact. Simulation applies assumptions only to a transient copy.

Every atom must be ground after $term resolution; costs must be finite and nonnegative. Abduction discards a rejected candidate, a direct target restatement and candidates contradicted by admitted base facts. Limits may leave the explanation search incomplete.

Executable examples

Single possible cause

@disk hypothesis
  holds disk_full server
  cost 2
  source "operator suggestion"

A possible disk condition is available to an abduction operation; it does not assert disk_full server.

Joint assumption

@joint hypothesis
  assume fan_stopped server
  assume temperature_high server
  cost 3

This is one indivisible candidate containing two ground assumptions; its cost applies to the whole candidate.

Invalid or unsafe use

@bad hypothesis
  holds disk_full ?machine

The parser accepts the atom, but lowering requires a ground assumption and rejects the unbound ?machine.

Knowledge language: hypothesis

In the knowledge language a hypothesis is a candidate assumption for abduction or a what-if; waive offers the relaxation of a norm. The tables and examples of this section are about the knowledge form, written from a source and checked by sop/knowledge/; the sections above describe the host-circuit form. Anchors of this section's fields start with kfield-. The language manual is the section "Knowledge wires" of DS004.

Other field names are rejected (unknown_field). The table is generated from sop/knowledge/grammar.mjs.

FieldCardinalityAccepted values and behavior
holdsAt most once; optionalOne ground atom the hypothesis assumes.
assumeRepeatable; optionalRepeatable ground atoms assumed together.
waiveRepeatable; optionalRepeatable $norm references: relaxing the norm is the hypothesis. Abduction over waive returns the minimal set of norms whose relaxation unblocks a plan, the negotiable item the host puts to the user.
costAt most once; optionalAn integer cost of making the assumption (a search preference, not a probability).
statusAt most once; optionaluntested, supported or rejected.
sourceAt most once; optionalQuoted citation.

What it is for

A hypothesis is never evidence: it is used by mode abduce (all inclusion-minimal explanations of an observation that are consistent with the admitted facts: a hypothesis whose consequences contradict a fact is reported under inconsistent, see Query modes) and offered by the host in the negotiation loop. A hypothesis needs holds, assume or waive (missing_field).

Examples

@a predicate
  args subject:entity
@act action
  params ?x
  requires a ?x
  adds a ?x
@n1 norm
  forbid ~act ?x
@h1 hypothesis
  waive $n1
  cost 1

Invalid: a hypothesis that assumes nothing

@h1 hypothesis
  cost 1
