lexeme

One lexicalization of one predicate in one language: the relation phrases that name it, the part of speech and the roles the surface realizes. Lexemes are what the KnowledgeLinker matches a relation phrase of a circuit against.

Status and author: Knowledge wire of a base memory, written next to the predicate it realizes by a person, a model or a mapping tool. The small model never authors it. The validator checks every form by linking it back through the relation linker.

Accepted fields

Single means at most once, repeatable means zero or more, and required means at least once. Other field names are rejected (unknown_field). The table is generated from the grammar in sop/knowledge/grammar.mjs.

FieldCardinalityAccepted values and behavior
ofExactly once; requiredThe id of the predicate this form realizes (unknown_predicate when the memory and its imports declare none).
languageExactly once; requiredA language code of two or three lowercase letters (bad_language); only en is accepted, the knowledge is English only (non_english_knowledge).
posAt most once; optionalOne of verb noun adj prep copula (default verb).
formRepeatable; requiredA JSON-quoted relation phrase in the convention of the model language: the lemma with its particles and prepositions, the copula included for copular phrases ("work at", "capital of", "be born in"). Every form must link back to its own predicate (form_does_not_link).
frameExactly once; requiredThe roles the surface realizes, in surface order, space separated: the grammatical subject first; a converse form lists the object role first ("parent of": object subject for a child predicate). A frame shorter than the predicate gives the warning frame_incomplete; a role the predicate does not have is frame_role_undeclared.
restrictRepeatable; optionalrestrict ROLE CLASS: the form applies only when the value of that role is of the class (restrict_class_unknown for an undeclared class). It separates predicates that share a form.
weightAt most once; optionalAn integer that orders two predicates sharing a form with the same frame when no restriction separates them.
sourceAt most once; optionalText: where the form comes from.
quoteAt most once; optionalThe exact words of the source, byte for byte.

What it is for and how to write it

The formalizer writes a relation phrase in the circuit-language convention (a lemma with its particles, "work at") and role names. The linker normalizes the phrase and the frame and looks for the lexeme whose form and role set match. Two predicates that share a form with the same frame length must say how they differ: a restrict line or distinct weight lines (ambiguous_form_undeclared), or the linker reports the phrase as ambiguous and asks.

Examples

@person entity
  kind class
  label en "person"
@organization entity
  kind class
  label en "organization"
@place entity
  kind class
  label en "place"

@works_at predicate
  args subject:entity object:entity
  role subject person
  role object organization
  label en "works at"

@lx_works_at_en lexeme
  of works_at
  language en
  pos verb
  form "work at"
  form "work for"
  frame subject object

Invalid: a language other than English

@works_at predicate
  args subject:entity object:entity
@lx_bad lexeme
  of works_at
  language ro
  form "lucra la"
  frame subject object

The knowledge is English only (DS014 "English-only core"): a message in another language is translated into English at the input edge, and answers are translated back at the output edge.

Invalid: a frame role the predicate does not have

@works_at predicate
  args subject:entity object:entity
@lx_bad lexeme
  of works_at
  language en
  form "work at"
  frame subject location

Invalid: a lexeme of a predicate that does not exist

@lx_bad lexeme
  of flies_to
  language en
  form "fly to"
  frame subject object

Common mistakes

  1. A frame that lists the roles in the wrong order for a converse phrase ("is the parent of" is subject first; "is a child of" for a parent predicate lists the object first).
  2. Declaring one lexeme per surface inflection: list lemma forms; inflection is handled by normalization.
  3. A shared form without restrict or weight.
  4. Repeating the same form in two languages: forms are per language.
