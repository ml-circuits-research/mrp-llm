reply

One phrasing of the assistant for a situation of a chat turn: a greeting, the answer to "thanks", the reply to a message without a request, the suggestion of a near name ("Did you mean …?"), the opening or closing that sets the tone of an answer, or a sentence of the answer renderer ("Answer: …", "Yes.", "Sources used: …"). The chat holds no phrasing in code: every sentence it says is a reply wire of the conversation layer config/knowledge/conversation-v1 (DS023 "Conversation layer").

Status and author: Knowledge wire of the conversation layer, written by the owner or a model and reviewed in /review like any other layer. A model-origin program cannot author it. It is data for the renderer, never evidence: the reasoning engines ignore it.

Accepted fields

Single means at most once, repeatable means zero or more, and required means at least once. Other field names are rejected (unknown_field). The table is generated from the grammar in sop/knowledge/grammar.mjs.

FieldCardinalityAccepted values and behavior
situationAt most once; requiredA lowercase symbol naming the situation the reply serves (courtesy_greeting, near_candidate, line_yes). Several reply wires of one situation and part are variants.
partAt most once; optionalopening, body (the default), closing, or line (a sentence of the answer renderer, chosen from the structure of the result packet).
languageAt most once; optionalThe language code; the knowledge is English only (non_english_knowledge), and the answer-formulation step phrases the final text in the user's language.
textAt most once; requiredA JSON-quoted text with {{slot}} placeholders (lowercase names) that the renderer fills from the result packet: answer, mention, candidate, candidate_description, relations, topics, readings, and the slots of the renderer lines. A malformed placeholder is bad_reply_slot.
sourceAt most once; optionalQuoted note of where the phrasing comes from.

How a reply is chosen

The chat turn maps its result packet to facts (cv_turn_status, cv_turn_unclear, cv_turn_signal, cv_turn_answered, the near-miss candidate, the memory's topics); the conversation layer reads its reply wires as the facts cv_reply_for, cv_reply_part and cv_reply_language, and its rules derive cv_applies SITUATION and cv_applicable_reply ?r. The JS oracle evaluates the layer in process; per part the applicable situation with the highest cv_situation_priority wins, one of its variants is picked with a seeded choice, and the derivation ("why this reply") goes to the packet field reply and the chat trace. Code only maps the packet to facts and fills the slots (lib/conversation/index.mjs).

Examples

@cv_courtesy_greeting_1 reply
  situation courtesy_greeting
  part body
  language en
  text "Hello! What would you like to know?"

@cv_courtesy_greeting_2 reply
  situation courtesy_greeting
  part body
  language en
  text "Hi there! What can I look up for you?"

@cv_near_candidate_1 reply
  situation near_candidate
  part body
  language en
  text "I don't have {{mention}} in my memory. Did you mean {{candidate}}?"

Two variants of the greeting reply and the near-miss suggestion; the renderer fills {{mention}} with the name of the message and {{candidate}} with the closest entity of the memory.

Invalid: a malformed placeholder

@bad reply
  situation near_candidate
  text "Did you mean {{Candidate}?"

Common mistakes

  1. Writing a sentence in code instead of a reply wire: every user-facing sentence belongs to the conversation layer.
  2. A slot the renderer cannot fill in that situation: the turn fails with reply_slot_missing, a configuration error.
  3. Choosing a reply with if/else in code: add a rule that derives cv_applies and a priority instead.
