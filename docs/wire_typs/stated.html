Wire index · assumed · unclear · query · unparsed · model/host roles

stated

A stated wire is the small model's formalized report of one clause that the user's message puts forward: an assertion, a supposition or a reported claim attached to the question. It belongs to the model language. The model writes stated only when it is sure the claim comes from the message; anything it adds, infers, completes or presupposes is assumed. When in doubt, assumed. One finite clause is one short wire; a subordinate clause is its own wire, related to its main clause by a link line. The normative contract is DS014.

The model has no context. Its prompt is the user's message only: no vocabulary, identifiers, knowledge or date. So a proposition is written as strings: a relation phrase, one line per role from a small closed inventory with the value as written in the message, the polarity, optional temporal expressions and the user's certainty. Content words are English, normalized: relation "work at" (the core is English-only: a Romanian or mixed message is translated into English before it reaches the model). After the model, the host links the strings to its reviewed lexicon: a phrase that does not link directly is retried with its English synonyms (config/dictionary/), the relation phrase and the set of role names select one predicate, the role values are resolved as entities with that predicate's argument types, and the temporal expressions are normalized. An unknown or ambiguous relation, entity or date becomes a host clarify; nothing is guessed. The result is the existing positional atom (works_at maria lab_alpha), so reasoners and oracles are unchanged. There is no quote, source or write field, and the statement is never recorded in the repository.

Anchoring and reports

There is no quotation field; the anchor to the message is checked mechanically by the agent guard: every quoted role value of a stated wire, and a non-user speaker, must appear in this turn's message after case, whitespace and accent folding, tolerating a small edit distance (0 for tokens up to 3 characters, 1 up to 7, otherwise 2, including an adjacent transposition) and a short inflected ending, or be an English synonym of words of the message ("Alpha Lab" for "the Alfa Laboratory"). Otherwise the turn is rejected with stated_value_not_in_message, and the model should use assumed, a query variable or an unparsed span instead. $id values and placeholders are structural and are anchored through the wires they name. The relation phrase is not anchored, because the model normalizes it. Exact duplicates are rejected (stated_duplicate); two contrary statements are accepted and the result reports the conflict. Every turn returns user_statements in the packet, each with the authored strings, the linked predicate and atom, validity, certainty, speaker, treatment (evidence or supposition) and a host-rendered statement such as You stated: works at (subject: Maria; object: Alpha Lab)., You supposed: … or According to Ana: …; a statement with links adds them, for example …, because: … [not checked].

A user assertion and a question

@s1 stated
  relation "works at"
  role subject "Maria"
  role object "Alpha Lab"
  polarity affirmed
  certainty asserted

@q query
  where match
    relation "work at"
    role subject ?who
    role object "Alpha Lab"
    polarity affirmed
  end
  select ?who

"Maria works at Alpha Lab. Who works at Alpha Lab?" The host links both phrases to works_at, resolves "Maria" and "Alpha Lab" with that predicate's types, and answers maria, not hypothetically: the user said it. The fact exists only for this turn's execution and the conversation context; it is never stored or reinforced.

A dated supposition

@s1 stated
  relation "works at"
  role object "Alpha Lab"
  role subject "Maria"
  polarity affirmed
  valid from "2025"
  valid until "2026"
  certainty supposed

@q query
  where match
    relation "works at"
    role subject "Maria"
    role object "Alpha Lab"
    polarity affirmed
  end
  at "June 2025"

"Suppose Maria worked at Alpha Lab during 2025. Was she there in June 2025?" Role lines may appear in any order. The host normalizes the years and the month; the answer is conditional (hypothetical: true) and the rendering lists the supposition.

Reported speech on one day

@s1 stated
  relation "door open"
  role source "room A"
  role destination "room B"
  polarity negated
  valid on "3 March 2026"
  certainty asserted
  speaker "Ana"

"Ana says the door between room A and room B was not open on 3 March 2026." The date is copied as written; the model has no clock and never converts it, the host normalizes it. Without a question the turn only reports the statement; reported claims and suppositions apply only to a question in the same message, and only asserted user statements are carried.

Borderline cases

The boundary rule: stated only when the claim surely comes from the message; anything else is assumed. "Since Nera left the lab, who runs it now?" → stated (an assertion embedded in a question). "Is Ana still at Acme?" → assumed with basis implicature (the user did not assert the earlier state). "Rooms A and B are booked. Is room C free?" → two stated plus an assumed basis closure for room C. "I think the door is open" → stated certainty hedged. "Is the bank open?" with two banks → the model writes "the bank"; the host finds two entities and asks. A name mentioned only in an earlier turn is not in this message: the earlier asserted statement is carried by the host, so the model does not restate it. More borderline cases, with executed examples, are in How the model writes.

First person

Facts about the user and the people the user names are formalized (DS014 Q-LANG-5, convention C4). The values are written "the user" and "the user's brother"; the host links "the user" to the caller's entity when the conversation supplies one. The user's own plans, feelings and chit-chat are left out. A modal stays in the relation ("be allowed to sign").

Clause links and arguments

A message with a subordinate clause gives one wire per clause. The main clause carries one keyword line per related clause, KEYWORD $id, with one of the ten link keywords (syntax). On a statement every link is recorded and reported (clause_links, status: "not_checked"); no engine checks a cause, a concession or a purpose, and the two propositions still execute. A statement that carries if or unless is conditional: it is reported only and never used as evidence.

EN “Maria works at Alpha Lab because she is on the Delta Project. Who works at Alpha Lab?”

@s1 stated
  relation "work at"
  role subject "Maria"
  role object "Alpha Lab"
  polarity affirmed
  certainty asserted
  because $s2

@s2 stated
  relation "be a member of project"
  role subject "Maria"
  role object "Delta Project"
  polarity affirmed
  certainty asserted

@q query
  select ?who
  where match
    relation "work at"
    role subject ?who
    role object "Alpha Lab"
    polarity affirmed
  end

The answer is maria from the user's evidence; the packet lists {"from": "s1", "keyword": "because", "type": "cause", "to": "s2", "status": "not_checked", "reason": "statement_link"} and the statement renders as "…, because: … [not checked]".

EN “The rollback failed because the migration is not idempotent.”

@s1 stated
  relation "fail"
  role subject "the rollback"
  polarity affirmed
  certainty asserted
  because $s2

@s2 stated
  relation "be idempotent"
  role subject "the migration"
  polarity negated
  certainty asserted

Two clauses, two wires; "because" is because on the main clause; the negation is polarity negated on the clause that says "not".

EN “Ana regrets that Maria left Alpha Lab.”

@s1 stated
  relation "leave"
  role subject "Maria"
  role source "Alpha Lab"
  polarity affirmed
  certainty asserted

@s2 stated
  relation "regret"
  role subject "Ana"
  role topic $s1
  polarity affirmed
  certainty asserted

The complement clause "that Maria left Alpha Lab" is its own wire, used as the topic argument of "regret" with $s1. The host reports s2 with its argument shown as "[leave]" and computes nothing over it.
